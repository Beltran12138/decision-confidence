# Evidence-adjusted leaderboard · October 2026 (No. 1)

Public AI trading leaderboards rank by return. This page asks a different question of the same data: **how much can each ranking tell you, and when could it first name a real winner?**

Method in one line: from each entry's published equity history, daily returns → annualised Sharpe → t = Sharpe × √(years of data). A single result needs t ≥ 2. Picking the best of N entries raises the bar (Bonferroni, one-sided): best of 41 needs about 3.26, best of 23,376 about 4.76. Where a board publishes no history, we say so.

\* Months of clean, forward history needed for the best entry to clear the best-of-N bar, assuming that entry's true Sharpe. Two caveats pull in opposite directions: counting every entry as an independent attempt overstates the selection charge when entries are correlated (parameter variants are), and the true Sharpe of any entry is unknown.

## Liquidity Arena, Phase I

Live sandbox trading, so closed-book by construction. The gap is length and selection: see [audit 003](../audits/003-liquidity-arena-phase-1.md). We will rescore Phase II when its results are published.

**Closest to the bar:** Kinance (rank 11), t = 1.63 on 31 days. If its Sharpe held at the observed 5.58, it would need about 4 months of forward record to clear the best-of-41 bar; one bad month would undo that. Ranked by evidence rather than return, the order changes: by t the top three are ranks 11, 7 and 17 (Kinance 1.63, btcol 1.53, Gamma Reasoning 1.49); the return leader, Little J, is fifth (1.42), and the gaps between them are far smaller than their noise.

**What would bring the date forward:** a longer forward window, or carrying Phase I history into Phase II so each team's record is longer than one phase.

## HKUDS AI-Trader

Data: `ai4trade.ai/api/profit/history?days=365&limit=50&include_history=true`, fetched 2026-10-12. The board ranks by cumulative return.

Three things stand out:

1. **The top of the board is mostly frozen.** Of the 34 top-50 agents with any history, 31 moved less than 1 percentage point between 24 July and 12 October, and 28 already had at least 90% of their current return when the recorded history begins on 8 July. Their ranks reflect gains made before any history the board publishes.
2. **The two highest-ranked agents publish no history at all.** #1 shows +271.6% on 18 trades; #2 shows +145.4%. With a single snapshot, neither a Sharpe nor a drawdown can be computed (the board's drawdown field reads 0.0 for both).
3. **The trial count is visible in the names.** Ten of the top 100 are variants of one naming scheme, `OC-rot-lb126-rb30-top2-pos0`, `OC-rot-lb252-rb30-top1-pos1`, `OC-tilt-lb126-rb30-soft2` and so on: lookback, rebalance period and number of holdings, swept as a grid. The board shows the winners of that sweep side by side with single entries, and ranks them the same way.

Within the 16 recorded days, none of the 32 agents with history reaches t = 2; the highest is 0.78. Sixteen days is far too short for any conclusion, in either direction.

Caveats: history is sparse (July only, then a live snapshot), so annualisation is rough; 46 of the top 100 show a trade count of 0 alongside non-zero returns, which we report as published and do not interpret.

**What would bring the date forward:** publish the full equity history for every ranked agent, rank on a fixed recent window rather than lifetime return, and group parameter variants from the same owner.
